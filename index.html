Braking

Brake in a straight line before the corner, then roll the throttle on as you unwind the steering. Turn the racing line on (L) to see where to brake: it turns red. Kerbs lift and shake the wheels on them, and two wheels on the grass pull the car toward it.

Slipstream

Tuck in behind another car on a straight for extra speed. SLIPSTREAM lights up above your speed when it's working.

Tuning

Each car has its own setups: open Tuning from the Garage, from your pit garage in practice and qualifying, or with T / Y on the grid's car select. Simple has seven plain controls; Realistic has every adjustment (wings, ride height, springs, bars, camber, toe, brakes, the diff, each gear). The panel beside it shows what a change does before you drive it. Changes save as you go; Saved keeps several setups per car and picks the one you race with.

Dirty Air

The same air costs you downforce in the corners: right behind someone, the front grips less and the car pushes wide. DIRTY AIR lights up when it's happening. Back off a little, or move off their line, until the straight.

Tyres

Soft is fastest but wears quickest, Medium and Hard last longer, Wet is for rain. Below 30% the tyre falls off a cliff.

Pit stops

Drive into the pit entry and the crew drives you to the box. Pick tyres and repairs with the arrows or d-pad on the way in.

Damage

Contact breaks wings (less grip) and the chassis (less speed). The car diagram next to your speed shows what's hurt. Repairs are done in the pits.

Drifting

Road and GT cars drift: flick the handbrake into a corner, then hold the angle with the throttle: more throttle for more angle, ease off to tighten, and counter-steer to trim it. A road car's limited-slip diff locks the rear wheels together on the power, which is what holds the angle (the GT racers need traction control off to hold one). Sideways is slower than gripping: a drift scrubs speed. Turn on Drift Points in Quick Race to score it.

Race weekends

Set Weekend in the race settings (Career and Grand Prix have it too): qualifying, or practice then qualifying, before the race. Sessions start in your garage: go out on a flying lap or down the pit lane, skip ahead, drive into the pits to come back in. Damage and worn tyres stay on the car: repairs and new tyres cost session time.

Delta

In qualifying and time trial the DELTA under your lap times compares you with your best lap as you drive: green, you're up on it; red, you're down.

Presets

Easy brakes and catches slides for you, Standard keeps ABS, traction and stability control, Sim turns everything off. Set each aid in Options › Aids, even mid-race from the pause menu.

The aid lights

The chips under the rev bar show the aids you have on. One flashes gold when it steps in: that's the car saving you, and a hint about what to do better.

ABS & braking

ABS stops the wheels locking. Without it, squeeze the brake: stamp on it and the fronts lock, skid, and won't steer until you ease off. Braking Assist brakes for you if you arrive at a corner too fast.

Traction & stability

TC cuts power when the rears spin up on the way out of a corner. SC brakes the outside front wheel and eases the power when the car starts rotating further than the tyres can hold. With both off, be gentle with the throttle.

Steering help

Counter-Steer Help points the wheels into a slide for you, and backs off when you counter-steer yourself. Steering Assist stops you turning in so hard the front tyres give up.

Prize bonus

In Career, every aid you turn off pays extra prize money, up to about +35% with Sim. The lowest setting you raced with counts, so turning aids back on mid-race lowers it.

Controller feel

With Vibration on you feel the fronts scrubbing, the rear sliding, wheelspin, ABS, lock-ups and kerbs. Set the stick's response, deadzone and speed in Options › Controller.

Online

The host can limit aids for everyone: no auto-brake or steering assist, or Sim only. It shows on the lobby's circuit card.

Yellow & green

Yellow: a car has spun, stopped or gone off. No overtaking in that sector, and slow right down for a double yellow. Green: all clear. Watch the flag board, the marshal panels and the minimap.

Give the place back

Passed someone under yellow or the VSC? Let them back past before the timer on the flag board runs out, or it's a 5 second penalty.

Blue flag

You're about to be lapped. Let the faster car through soon. Hold them up for too long and it's a penalty. AI cars move over for you the same way.

Virtual Safety Car

After a big crash or a car stuck on the track. Everyone slows down. Keep the delta on the flag board positive (green) and don't overtake. Pitting under the VSC costs less time.

Black & orange

A wing has come off. Pit for repairs within 2 laps. Ignore it and it's a drive-through (Realistic rules: the black flag).

Track limits

Cutting the inside of a corner deletes that lap, and in a race it's a warning. Running wide is forgiven on Arcade unless you go well off and keep your speed. Realistic: the white line is the limit. Too many warnings: black and white flag, then penalties.

Collisions

Run into the back of a car and the stewards take a look. If they spin, go off or get damaged, it's a 5 second penalty. Side-by-side contact is a racing incident.

Penalties

Time: served at your next stop or added at the flag. Drive-through: drive through the pits within 3 laps. Set the strictness with Race Rules, and how often the AI slips up with AI Mistakes.

A world that keeps going

World Career has no end. Every driver ages, gets quicker, peaks and retires; rookies come up through the series. The other series race every round too, whether you're in them or not.

The ladder

Street Cup, GT Championship, Formula SV. Finish a season in the top three and the series above offers your team a place. You can take it or stay.

The computer

Between rounds you're in your garage. Sit down at the desk (or click the monitor). Three things on it: Mail; Team, with tabs for an overview, the car, tyres, staff and session data; and the Internet: the news, the series' websites, the driver database, the classifieds, parts, jobs and the bank. A new career starts here too: register the team, claim your winnings, buy a car.

Money

You start with your lottery winnings: a lump sum now, or a yearly annuity (more in total, less tax). Every round costs money: entry, travel, the crew, each set of tyres (new sets in practice too), fuel by the lap and repairs. Prize money comes back, more with fewer driver aids. The Bank is on the computer.

The car

Your car is six parts (engine, gearbox, brakes, suspension, aero, chassis), each wearing with every round. Worn parts are slower and can fail in a race. Rebuild or replace them on the computer (Team: the Car tab). Upgrades come from Speed Parts Direct or used off SVList (on the Internet): you'll feel the difference, and a data engineer can tell you what it really is.

Tyres

Order tyres ahead of each round (Team: the Tyres tab, on the computer). In the pit garage, Tyres shows your sets: new ones in stock and used ones you took off earlier. Run out at a weekend and the trackside service charges 50% more. A team manager orders for you.

Sponsors

Offers arrive by email: local businesses from your home town first, bigger names as your standing grows (results, met targets, clean racing). A title sponsor's name goes on the car by itself; up to two associates need their logo on it too (Team: Sponsors, put it on the car, or upload your own version of their logo). No logo, no money, and they won't wait long. Cars that only take a paint colour carry sponsors as window stickers. Each pays a fee every round and a bonus when you meet their target for that race (shown before the race and on the Team app's Sponsors tab). Miss too many and they leave. Deals run to the end of the season; good ones offer to renew.

Drivers

Every driver has a trait or two (digger, loyal, hothead, rain master, mentor, metronome, pay driver). Sign your teammate on the Team app's Drivers tab: free agents any time, anyone in your series and below between seasons. Offer their ask or lowball them. Drivers remember: late pay, dropping someone mid-season and dirty driving the stewards miss (forcing people off, bump and runs, brake tests) hurt your reputation, and the driver you wronged holds a grudge: they race you harder, tell the press, and won't sign for you.

Staff

Hire a team manager (cheaper travel), a chief mechanic (cheaper repairs, quicker stops) and a data engineer (without one the session data and the lap review are unreadable) from the Team app's Staff tab (hiring is on SVJobs). Payroll runs as each round starts: pay people on time, half, or hold it. Unpaid staff get unhappy, are owed the rest, and eventually walk out.

Your phone

Texts from your teammate and rivals arrive between rounds. Press P (Y on a pad) in the garage to raise it.

Host a game

Pick Host Game to open a room. Send the 5-letter room code to your friends. You choose the circuit, laps, AI and weather in the lobby.

Join a game

Type the room code on the Online screen and press Enter. Mark yourself Ready in the lobby.

In the race

Online races can't be paused: the menu opens while your car slows down. Session points are kept for every race you do together.

Same version

Everyone needs the same copy of the game. If your friend is on Windows, send them the single-file build.
